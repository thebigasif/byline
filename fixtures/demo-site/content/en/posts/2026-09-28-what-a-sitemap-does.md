A sitemap is a file, usually at `/sitemap.xml`, that lists the addresses of the pages on your site. Search engines read it to find pages they might otherwise miss.

## What it does

- It helps new pages get found sooner, especially on a young site with few links pointing in.
- It tells search engines when a page last changed, so they know what to fetch again.
- It points to each language version of a page, which helps the right reader land on the right version.

## What it does not do

A sitemap does not make a page rank. A weak page listed in a sitemap is still a weak page. It also does not replace links: a page that only appears in the sitemap, with no links from your own posts, still looks unimportant. See [why internal links matter](/blog/why-internal-links-matter/).

## Keep it clean

List only pages you want in search results. Leave out drafts, redirects and pages marked `noindex`. A sitemap full of pages that should not be there teaches search engines to trust it less.
